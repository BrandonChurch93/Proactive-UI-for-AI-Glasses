# Overlay Spec · the glasses layer

The overlay renders the brain's decisions. It owns placement, motion and the light vocabulary. It takes `Decision[]` and a camera; it never scores anything. Material, timings and moves are defined in `docs/design-system.md`; this document is how they are wired.

## 1. Shape

```
packages/overlay
  src/
    index.ts           mount(sceneBridge, root): Overlay
    panes/             label, glance, card, expanded, toast, strip, ask, reminder, followup, pocket, whisper
    ground/            route band, turn arc, destination marker, safety text
    light/             pip, aura, flare, head, leader, seed, edge light, trace
    place.ts           projection, speed-scaled focus zone, dynamic keep-outs, yielding, step-aside, hysteresis, edge folding, below-anchor fallback, scale by distance
    backdrop.ts        applies the sampled luminance to pane alpha and weight (walking rule 9)
    motion.ts          GSAP vocabulary: mark, gaze, travel, condense, dissolve, retract, open, close, recede, return, batch, yield, step aside, auto-fold, save, surface, whisper, snooze, edge fold
    sound.ts           synthesized sounds
    hud.ts             the recede container
```

Two render layers:
- **WebGL** (inside the scene, bloomed): pip, aura, flare, head, leader, destination column, route band and safety text are world geometry or sprites. They are added to the scene through a small bridge (`addLight(obj)`, `project(v3)`, `unprojectAtDepth(x,y,z)`), so the overlay package never imports the sim.
- **DOM** (above the canvas): panes, message banners, the held glyph, the ask glance, the menu button and the menu, the map tile (a canvas drawn every frame from the bridge's `mapView`), reviewer chrome. Positioned every frame from projected anchors. Panes use `backdrop-filter`; they are plain HTML and CSS so copy and layout stay editable without touching the renderer.

## 2. Input

```ts
overlay.apply(decisions: Decision[], t: number)
```

The overlay diffs against what is on screen and runs the matching move: a new `show` at label or glance → mark (if no pip yet, and only for proactive content; a label that answers a look or a settle condenses with its light) then travel and condense; `promote` → grow the same surface; `demote` that keeps a pane → shrink it; `demote` to a pip → dissolve, retract; `hold` while shown → nothing until a gate; gate on → recede; gate off → return and the held reason line; `save` → the save bloom and the glyph count; `surface` → the pocket pane; a `whisper` level → the whisper line. The overlay also reports back, through the bridge, the rect of every visible pane so the sim can sample the backdrop behind it, and it keeps a log of what the glass actually did: every pane birth, resize, move over 40 px, drop, hide, edge fold, step-aside and death, with its cause and rect, keyed by sim time. The walk stores the log beside the brain frames, exports it, and the inspector interleaves it with the brain's decisions.

Moves are queued per anchor so two decisions on the same object never fight. Recede interrupts anything.

## 3. Placement, per frame

1. Project the anchor. A pane whose anchor leaves the view folds at once (Edge fold); a light whose anchor is behind the camera or off-screen for more than 250 ms shows as the edge light, clamped to the nearest edge at the anchor's height. Skip the rest.
2. Scale: `clamp(22 / distance, .7, 1)`, times the viewport scale (design-system 5, Screen scale: 0.74 to 1.9), never below 1, so the smallest pane text (12 px at scale 1) never draws smaller at any size or distance: full size at 22 m and nearer, 0.7 at 31 m and beyond on screens large enough for that to stay above 12 px. The scale is chosen with the configuration and kept with it: a move never changes it, and inside the 1.2 s hysteresis a re-chosen configuration keeps the current scale too (no shrinking toward 0.74, a lift at the same scale), so a pane changes scale at most once per 2.5 s.
3. Positions, in order: beside the light on the side away from the view center, the pane's near edge 24 px from the light and rising from it, the light 85 % of the way down the pane's height so a tall view clears the sign the light sits on; then the same on the side toward the center; then centered above the light, its bottom edge 22 px above it; then centered below. A pane beside its light that would run into the glass's chrome above it (a banner, the clock) slides down its side until it clears it, as long as the light stays 12 px or more below the pane's top edge. Each is tried at its scale and then shrunk toward 0.74. A position fits only if keeping it in the view moves it 24 px or less; otherwise the pane folds (step 9). The pane on the thing being looked at ignores the focus zone. Any other pane must also clear the zone; if none of the three does, it may be pushed just outside the zone only while it stays within its own width of its light, and that pushed spot then moves with the store; otherwise it waits as its light (`hiddenBy: room`) until there is room or it is looked at.
4. Reject positions over the view-zone panes (the message banners, the menu, the map tile) and the reviewer chrome (Sound, Pause, `I · INSPECTOR`, the pace readout, the held glyph), the clock and the menu button, each padded 8 px, over the roadway, over the projected bounds of every vehicle (moving or stopped in traffic) and person between the eye and the pane's anchor, the path band, and face bounds. If no position clears the roadway, the pane sits centered above its light with its bottom at the roadway's top edge. Keep-outs are recomputed every frame; an overlap with a person or vehicle that lasts 120 ms (a face or a moving vehicle at once) triggers the step-aside move (walking rule 8). The roadway only rejects positions; it never triggers a step-aside. A step-aside lands only where it clears the chrome as well as the movers.
   - A mover's keep-out is its box projected with every edge clipped at a 0.3 m near plane, its silhouette clipped to the view, then capped at 60% of the view in each dimension, trimmed from the side that runs off the view. It counts for a pane only when its nearest point is nearer than that pane's anchor.
   - The roadway is the road surface between the curbs (the avenue and the cross street) from 2 m to 60 m ahead of the eye, cut at 48% of the view height so the far street near the horizon never blocks the sky above a far store, and split into four horizontal bands that follow its perspective.
5. Yielding: within 8 m of a curb, a head turn faster than 0.8 rad/s toward a pane's side runs the yield move. A yielding pane's rect is frozen where it was when the yield began; it stays yielded until the scan ends (the head is back within 0.3 rad of where the scan began, or the look settles on a different place), and then placement resumes with a 320 ms ease from the frozen spot (walking rule 5).
6. Backdrop: apply the sampled luminance from the snapshot to the pane's fill alpha and weight (walking rule 9).
7. Hysteresis: a placed position is kept while it stays valid. The corner the pane grows from follows its light with a 4 px dead band on reprojection jitter; a change of more than 80 px in where the pane sits relative to its light animates over 320 ms, and a re-placement drifts over `t-drift` (900 ms) `ease-travel`, unless the path would cross the focus zone for a pane that is not looked at. A move, a re-placement or the 80 px rope, starts no more than 0.9 of the pane's width from where it lands, so in a fast head turn it never trails its light. During a head scan at the curb (rule 5) nothing is re-placed: a pane stays with its store, and one that has ended up somewhere it could not normally be yields to 15% with the scan.
8. Edge collapse, for panes not looked at (including the one the look has just left): card within 8% of the edge → label; label within 4% → it folds. A new pane is born at its placed position with the condense move (walking rule 4).
9. Fold before the cut: a pane folds over `t-quick` when keeping it in the view would move it more than 24 px, when any part of it would lie outside the view, when it would cover the reviewer chrome now or within the next 0.4 s at its current speed (a label riding up beside a near storefront keeps moving while it fades), or when its anchor leaves the view. The fold fades the pane where it stands (it no longer follows its anchor); the edge light shows only when the anchor itself is off the view. A pane moving to a new place (a re-placement's drift) is tested against the chrome where it lands, not on the way there. A fold clears any move in flight. A folded pane stays folded until it fits again 40 px clear of every view edge (the pane being looked at by a wearer standing still needs only the 16 px margin, since at a door the doorway can push it close to the edge); a pane the look has just left stays folded. No pane is ever drawn partly outside the view above 20% opacity.

The card's live line (`content.live`) carries the brain's `liveTone` when the place's data gives one: the dot takes the tone's color and the status after the line's first interpunct is set in it (`Uptown 4 min · ` in ink, `elevator out` in `--warn`), the fact before it stays in ink; without a tone the dot is `--go` and the line is ink, as before.

The expanded view grows in place from the card's position and is placed by the same rules with a larger box. It never docks to a screen edge and never changes the world.

## 4. Input model (sim side, but the overlay consumes it)

- Drag turns the head, a full turn with no limit: the head turns 360° and wraps, and looking back works like looking to the side (the head-check rules in `docs/walking-rules.md` are what a turn past the shoulder is for). The drag moves the head 1:1 with the pointer, a full view width equals 120° at every size, with no inertia and no easing. A head is on a body: the drag turns the head relative to the body 1:1 at every moment, corners included, and the view swings with the body while the wearer drags exactly as it does while they hold still. The viewfinder at the center of the view is the gaze target (design-system 9).
- Dwell: 250 ms on a place lights its label, and 0.5 s more of hold grows it into the card (walking rule 10). The brain receives `gaze.dwellMs` and `gaze.settled`; the overlay only shows the tell: aura and flare, the viewfinder's corners, and the held pane drawn into the center. Panes are placed in priority order each frame (the place under the center, then cards, then labels nearest first), and each pane placed is a hard rect, padded 6 px, for the ones after it, so labels never overlap; a label with no room waits as its light. A hidden pane keeps its measured size for placement, so it does not flicker between fitting and not.
- Pinch: a click with under 4 px of movement skips the wait on the gazed item or picks a choice; clicking a pane directly also works. Tap on touch. While the band is Walk or Brisk a pinch saves to the pocket instead.
- Choosing in an expanded view: where the rows are each a choice, exactly one row is lifted, the one under the look or the pointer, else the row marked now, else the first choice, and the primary action reads for it; a tap lifts a row on a touch screen. While the view is open at Stopped or Stroll and the look is on the place or the view, the view holds its place in the world as every held pane does (its offset from the projected light is kept, so it moves on the glass exactly as the world does), the head can carry the viewfinder across its rows, and the look stays on the place while the center is within the view plus 80 px; 1 s outside that, the view goes back to its placement. Its light leaving the glass as the head travels the rows is no cause to let it go, and while it is read (a drag working its rows, or at Stopped the center on it) neither the chrome nor a keep-out re-places it, since a re-place moves the rows from under the eye; a drag that carries it farther from the center than that drag has yet brought it (by more than 6 px, a standing wearer's sway) is a turn away, and then it lets go and rides like any held pane. A pinch (a click on the street, Enter, the space bar while such a view is under the look, a tap) or a click on the action takes the action for the lifted row. The choice event carries the row (`{ id: <place>, action: <primary>, row: <key or title> }`); the scripted reviewer sends it as a `choose` beat with a `row`.
- Snooze: looking up past 0.55 rad at nothing for 2 s, the head held still for the last second; the glyph reads "Snoozing" meanwhile. Looking up again the same way cancels.
- Dismiss: right-click, Esc, or looking away for 1 s per level.
- Menu: the button at the bottom right opens it (a click, or the pointer resting on it 0.5 s); a category, then a place, which becomes the destination; Back and Close; Esc closes it. The scripted reviewer drives it with choice events on `menu` (`open`, a category, a place id). A category's places keep the order they had when the list opened. View, alongside it: the map tile, never a gaze target, drawn heading-up along the walk: the plan turns with the route's tangent at the wearer's place on it (`pointAt` on `sim.route`), eased over `t-base` and held while the wearer is Stopped, and the view cone is the head's direction relative to that up (design-system 6, Map tile).
- Keys: Enter pinches (expands, or chooses in an expanded view), Esc dismisses, I opens the inspector, Space pinches while an expanded view with an action is under the look and pauses otherwise.

## 5. Ground layer

- Route band: 0.46 m wide, additive, fades to zero 25 m ahead; drawn as quads in the scene so it sits on the pavement and takes perspective. Draws on from the feet over 1.2 s `power2.out`.
- Turn arc at the corner, 2.4 m radius.
- Destination column: 26 m tall gradient at the door, settles to a pip on arrival over 1.2 s.
- Safety text: WALK · WAIT countdown and BIKE · Ns as a world-space plane at the curb line in Martian Mono, tone by state. The pavement is the only place safety lives.
- During a gate the route dims to 40% and stays.

## 6. Accessibility and legibility

- Every pane passes 4.5:1 for primary text and 3:1 for secondary against the darkest and brightest façades on the block, with backdrop adaptivity applied (test both, and record the sampler's luminance in the test).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: travel and flare are skipped, condense becomes a 200 ms fade, recede, return and yield keep their timing (they are safety).
- Focus is visible on any interactive element; the whole demo is keyboard operable.

## 7. As built

- Gaze: the viewfinder is the target, and the target is the viewfinder. In live play a place is wherever the wearer can read it or see it: its pane (any level, on the glass above 50% opacity; a pane the world has dimmed below that still keeps the look but cannot take it), its light, or the front of its storefront (a storefront from about 0.3 m above the pavement to just above the sign, a place with no frontage by the upright from 0.9 m to its sign). A place is acquired when any of the three is inside the 160 px square (times `max(1, --ui)`); with nothing held, a pane or a light within 48 px of the square's edge is caught as if inside (`VIEWFINDER.catchPx`), so aiming does not need a dead-center hit. A held place keeps the look while any of the three is inside the keep square, the viewfinder plus 40 px (40 px beyond the catch for a place the catch took), and a pane the hold itself placed counts as inside it wherever it stands, so a pane set just past its light (design-system 5) never lets the look go. That is the label's rule. A card or an expanded view keeps the look only while the viewfinder stays on it (the owner's rule, Phase 30): the frame the center of the view leaves its rect, or a turn carries it, riding with the world, more than half the held square (`VIEWFINDER.held` / 2, 66 px at 1280 x 800, times `max(1, --ui)`) from where it stood when the wearer was last at rest (the pointer up and no turn for 0.1 s; `travelRef` in `Walk`), or the turn itself is that large (the head's change in angle, in pixels of the view), since walking past a shop at 4 m can carry the pane along with a turn, as long as the pane ends farther from the center than it started (a turn that brings it toward the center is aiming at it); the head's part is judged by where the turn carries the pane from where it stood, not by where the glass draws it afterwards (a card the overlay collapses to its label for want of room is drawn near its light, and judged by that rect a turn off Atlas Books looked like no turn at all), the look is let go with no release grace of its own, its light and storefront counting for nothing. The center can only leave a pane it has been on: a card grown from a storefront look can be born off the center, and until the pull brings it under the viewfinder it keeps the look as a label does, the pane inside the keep square, while a turn still ends it (`Walk.onCard`; holding it to its rect from birth made the look flicker in the bar's probes, the problem Phase 29 met). A pane counts as a card or a view by the brain's level, not by what the glass draws: a card the overlay draws collapsed to its label for want of room is a card still, and under the label's rule it kept the look by its light after a turn. So is a card the world hides (rule 8 drops it to its light when a vehicle covers the light): while it is hidden the look rests on it as long as the wearer does not turn away, judged by the head's turn from where the pane last stood, and its storefront and light keep nothing (Atlas Books, hidden by the truck mid-turn, kept the look by its storefront). the brain's 0.5 s look-away is the only grace, so it shrinks to its label half a second after. Dimmed or not makes no difference. A place the look has left is kept in `Walk.leftLook` while the brain has a pane up for it, and is taken again only as a turn lands on it, within the aiming window (0.5 s after the last turn): its pane brought under the center by a drag, from off it, or its light inside the viewfinder (the half, not the catch square) as the turn ends; nothing else brings it back, not its storefront, not the catch square, not the world or the glass carrying its pane back under the center after the drag (the reviewer's Pocket Park, 23 px off after the turn and walked back over the center), and the 60 px takeover gives it nothing. A card or a view under the center takes the look dimmed by rule 8 or not, since it has no light to fall back on. `Walk.lastTake` records the last change of the look and what made it, for the live checks. Generally, a storefront lands a look only for a place with no pane up, and only while the wearer turns or within the aiming window after: resting on a building takes nothing. At the destination's door the Phase 29 rule stands, since the wearer stood there to read: while they turn the view keeps the look by its rect plus 24 px (`READ.marginPx`, `lookOnPane`), the door's light in the viewfinder, and the door's upright (the pavement to the light) in the viewfinder, as its label does, at rest as a label does, and the rows are read by moving the square down it; a door's place is not put on the left list. A place's distance is the nearest of its pane, its light and its storefront to the center of the view, and the nearest place wins: a light within 30 px of the center outranks a storefront that merely contains it, because aiming at a light is precise, and a pane under the center outranks a light behind it by the same 30 px, because the pane is what the wearer aims at; of two panes under the center the smaller wins, the finer aim. While the head is held still nothing nearer takes a held place; during a turn, or within a second after one, only a place whose pane or light has been within 60 px of the center for 250 ms in a row takes it (`VIEWFINDER.aimPx`), and only if it is clearly nearer the center (by a pixel or more) than the held place was when the wearer's turn ended, its dwell counted from when it became the candidate, so the place a turn acquires is the place aimed at and the glass drawing a held pane into the center never hands the look to whatever is left under the center. Over a held expanded view under the center, another place's pane under the center is the aim outright, the margin not applying, since two panes both under the center otherwise differ only by the smaller-pane tie-break. The look landing on a place pins its pane where it stands, so nothing re-places it; once the look has held 250 ms and the wearer rests, the pull draws it toward the center (design-system 5), and the corners pull in to 132 px while it is held (design-system 9). While the wearer drags the view, and for half a second after letting go, every other pane already on the glass rides with the world where it stands instead of being re-placed, so the one being aimed at is exactly where the turn meets it, and it cannot be crowded off the glass by a held pane the turn carries across it; a pane the ride carries off the glass folds at the edge, as a pane whose light leaves the view does, rather than being held to the glass and sliding along its edge against the world; once the drag is over each goes back to its placement in one 320 ms move. The overlay reads the look every frame from the sim (`gaze` in its options), not from the last brain tick, so a pane is pinned before the tick that marks it looked at can re-place it. A place the wearer's own turn carries out of the square is let go at once; one the body's steps carry out keeps its hold for the release grace (0.5 s, the brain's `lookAwayS`; one number in `LOOK_AWAY_S`), and a place that comes back within that half second resumes its hold where it was rather than starting again. Once a place the wearer turned onto is acquired (during a turn or within a second of one), the head holds it from the first frame, and the pursuit follows the held place and no other, never a place still being weighed: it counter-rotates for every move, sidestep and turn of the body, so the aim survives the body's steps around people and street furniture; only a deliberate turn away ends the hold, and the head stops following once the place is 120° behind the walking direction. The hold acts only while the pointer is up; the moment it goes down the drag owns the head, and the hold resumes when it comes up. A place the walk carries into the square while nobody is aiming is looked at but does not pull the head after it. Any place in view within 70 m can be looked at; the brain grows a label beyond 35 m only after a second of hold, grows nothing until the head has moved 5° since the walk or the lap began, and gives a place beyond 30 m a compact card, and the full card arrives in place as the wearer closes in. The scripted reviewer aims exactly, keeps its 90° turn, and uses the sim's 3.5° cone. Over a view-zone pane (a banner, the pocket's rows, the menu and its rows) the pointer is the gaze, since those panes have no place in the world to turn toward; a menu row under the pointer is the gaze target (`pick:<id>`) and a click is the pinch that chooses it; the rows scroll with the wheel or a drag past six. The menu button is a gaze target the same way: the viewfinder cannot reach chrome fixed to the glass, so the pointer resting on it 0.5 s opens the menu, as a click does. The map tile is never a gaze target. A destination's light stands at its door (`door:<id>`), and a look at the door is a look at the place: the live gaze reports the place, never the door as a target of its own. At its door the destination's light outranks any other place's pane: inside the square it wins, and within 60 px it takes a held place over in a turn, so a neighbour's pane held near the center never keeps the look from the door the wearer walked to (the reviewer's station stop and a Mega Burger stop in Phase 27 both failed that way, the look left on another place for the whole stop). While a place's expanded view lies under the center of the view, or within 24 px of it, the look stays on that place (the wearer is reading its rows), and nothing behind the pane takes it; this is the card and view rule above. Over an expanded view the pointer is not the gaze (the place keeps the look); it lifts the row it rests on.
- Placement is the shared solver in `packages/shared/src/placement.ts` and `placement-state.ts`, run by the overlay and by the headless recorder alike. Hysteresis keeps a pane's position, alignment and shrink until it stops being valid (it leaves the view, it would sit over the roadway, or for a pane not looked at, it enters the focus zone).
- Keep-outs carry a 0.45 s lookahead along each mover's velocity. A face or a moving vehicle acts on the first frame; a body after 120 ms. The live overlay and the headless recorder build keep-outs with the same code (`keepOuts` in `packages/sim/src/headless.ts`, geometry in `packages/shared/src/keepout.ts`) and place panes with the same `Placement` and `Aside` (`packages/shared/src/placement-state.ts`), so recorded walks carry the rects the glasses would show. A pane that cannot slide clear (a truck filling the view) dissolves to its pip at 45% and condenses again 300 ms after the way is clear. View-zone panes are exempt from stepping aside: they live at the edges by design and have no pip to fall back to.
- Each dissolve runs on its own timeline so a newer move on the same anchor never strands a half-dissolved pane.
- Strips (app updates) arrive with the Batch move rather than a condense, and an anchor leaving the view fades its pane over t-quick (Edge fold) rather than dissolving it.
- View zone: message banners at the top center, 20 px under the clock line, one at a time with a second text tucked 6 px under it as a 36 px strip on the same glass (placed from the banner's drawn bottom, in the live overlay and the headless report alike); the menu and the map tile at the right edge above the menu button (the map 24 px above it, the menu 16 px above it, the map hidden while the menu is open); the ask glance and the pocket as before; the top-right slot, 56 px down to clear the inspector control, is free for anything that needs the top.
- Ground: the route band is a soft-edged ribbon (0.46 m with a 0.14 m core) that draws on from the feet; the safety text lies on the crosswalk 1.9 m past the curb along the crossing, reading in the crossing direction, and during the crossing it travels 5.5 m ahead of the wearer so the countdown stays in view through the recede, never closer than 1.2 m to the far curb so it never lands on the sidewalk; it sits on a soft dark under-glow so it reads on white paint as well as asphalt; the bike warning lies 5.5 m ahead of the wearer (nearer than that it falls below the bottom of the view at eye height) and takes the countdown's place while it shows; the destination column fades as the wearer comes within 14 m of the door and settles to a pip 2.6 m above the door.
- The arrival line is anchored at the door at head height (`door:<id>`), not the sign, so it is in view when the wearer is at the door. A pane on a door anchor is placed with the doorway (`doorwayRect`: the opening from the pavement to 2.2 m, 1 m either side of the door point, projected) as its own keep-out: its side position is pushed 40 px out past the opening as far as 0.95 of its width from the light allows, and no position may cover the middle half of the opening, and walkers ignored by the placement (rule 8 handles the ones that cross it); the live overlay and the headless recorder pass the same rect.
- GSAP is driven by the sim clock (`gsap.updateRoot`), so stills, pauses and seeks show exactly the frame the timing implies.
- Viewport scale and step-down: see design-system 5 (as built). Placement passes the scale into the shared solver, so the focus zone, keep-outs and edge rules are unchanged at every size.
- Touch: drag turns the head, a tap pinches, a long-press (550 ms without moving) dismisses, and a Pause control sits beside Sound. A phone held upright pauses behind the rotate prompt and resumes when turned.
- Accessibility: whatever condenses onto the glass is also announced to a screen reader through a polite live region; the canvas is labelled; opening the inspector moves focus into it and closing returns it.
- Stability: a change of level that comes only from placement (near the screen edge, off view, not enough room) must hold for 800 ms before it happens, and never happens while the pane is yielding to a head scan. Without this, dragging the view or a head scan flipped a pane between card, glance and edge several times a second, and each flip restarted the reveal so the pane never appeared. Level changes the brain decides, and the drop to a pip for a vehicle, stay immediate.
- One surface: each item has one pane element from birth to death. A level change (from the brain, an edge collapse or a step-down) or a change in the content's structure measures the new content off-screen and morphs the same element (design-system 4, Grow and Shrink). Placement holds the corner the pane grows from, not its top-left, so the rope's 4 px dead band and a re-placement's drift act on that corner and a resize never moves it. The one exception is a resize that no longer fits its place (an expanded view with no room above): it takes its new place at once, as it did before.
- Leader: a two-point line from the pip to the nearest point of the pane edge nearest the pip (8 px in from its corners), recomputed every frame, so beside a pane the line stays level and short. Its pane end is placed in the scene at most 6 m from the eye on the ray through that point, so the line is straight on screen, depth-tested and bloomed.
- Live gaze picks the place nearest the center among those inside the viewfinder (above). A storefront counts only if its projected outline is at least 36 px wide and faces the view within 75° (a frontage seen nearly edge-on is a sliver that would cover the vanishing direction); its light still counts. A place lights only after 250 ms of gaze, so sweeping the view across a row of shops lights nothing.
- The held pane (`Overlay.hold`), the owner's rule since 2026-10-31: drawn into the viewfinder. The frame the look lands on a pane, the hold is made and it is the only hold there is: the pane's center keeps an offset from its projected light (the light its placement was made against, carried to this frame's), fixed exactly where the pane was drawn the frame before, riding or placed, carried to this frame's light, with the step aside and head-scan yield it was drawn with set apart because the draw goes on adding them (`Item.lastShift`); a move under way (a re-place ease, a freeze released) stops at that frame rather than taking one more step, so the drawn rect does not change between the frame before the pin and the frame of it (Phase 29 pinned from the placement's corner at the drawn size, and a re-place ease under way still moved a label 22 px on its pin frame in Phase 30's aim; Phase 28's rect-center pin moved the Post Office's 22 px). A pane a head scan froze rides, when a drag begins, from where the scan left it, and the freeze ends without its ease back, and nothing moves it until the pull begins: no come beside the square (retired in Phase 28 with `besideSquare` and its test, and with it the square as a keep-out for the looked-at pane), no growth shift, no chrome nudge at rest, no head-scan yield. The pull begins once the look has held 250 ms and the pane has finished appearing (its condense fade at 0.9), while the wearer rests on it (the pointer up and half a second past, the head turning slower than 1.5 rad/s, the light within a pane's width of the square): the offset is driven toward its target by a critically damped step each frame (`PULL_W` = 5.8 / `t-pull`), so it starts softly, settles in about 1.4 s and never passes the target. The target is the view's center; at a door it moves sideways, the least that keeps the pane 40 px clear of the doorway's middle half and inside the view, because the opening is where people walk in and out; and it is the nearest point to that, searched outward in 8 px rings to 480 px, where the pane is inside the view and 20 px (`MOVE.asidePad`) clear of the chrome, every other pane as last drawn (whatever order a frame places them in), every face and every person or vehicle that is not standing still. Not resting, the velocity is zeroed and the offset held: the pane rides with the world, a turn leaves it behind, and nothing follows the head. While the pull moves it faster than 20 px/s the frame is marked `moved: held`. It grows about its own center and is never moved for it. Not at rest, a pane that would touch the chrome takes the least shift that clears it, within 56 px in any direction, in place, or stays. Rule 8 still acts on it as on any pane, but a pulled pane it cannot step aside dims instead of dropping to its light (the pull carries it off), and rule 5's yield passes it by. An expanded view the wearer drags across (a drag with the center on the view or within the 24 px read margin of it; a drag elsewhere is a turn) is marked `reread` and the pull rests for the rest of the look, so the rows stay where the drags put them; the reading rules below still hold it through the drags. Carried off the glass with its light a pane's width from the square it folds where it stands. A look landing on a place whose placement has it at its pip or folded at the edge brings it back just above its light, and the pull draws it in from there. A drag that ends on the looked-at pane leaves it held where it rode. Once the look has been gone 1 s it hands back to placement from where it stands and drifts to its place over `t-drift` (900 ms) `ease-travel`, the distance uncapped. Placement's own re-places drift the same way. A ride begun by the pointer going down takes its offset against the light its placement was made with, so the first frame of a drag does not leave the pane behind on the glass.
- Hysteresis for the looked-at pane: once placed it leaves its place, for the edge or for want of room, only for a cause that has lasted 300 ms (`MOVE.leaveMs`), the placement holding its last rect moved with the anchor meanwhile; the anchor leaving the view and the chrome act at once. The chrome look-ahead (0.4 s along the pane's motion) applies only to panes nobody is looking at, since the jitter of a held pane beside a banner made it fold and return every 100 ms.
- Visibility: `report()` lists every item the overlay holds, pips included, with `visible` and `hiddenBy` (`keepout` after a drop, `edge` when the anchor is off view, `offscreen` when it is behind the eye, `room` when the pane cannot be placed). The pane on the thing being looked at never edge-collapses and never steps down a level: away from the edge it shrinks to 0.74 and lifts above its anchor, near the edge it folds (step 9), and it reports `room` only if neither works. A pane that is hidden (dropped, folded to the edge, no room) keeps its element and condenses back in place; the overlay log calls that a `return`, so a pane is born once per life.
- Step aside slides a pane at most its own width. It prefers up or down, because people and vehicles cross the view sideways, and it never slides the way the mover is travelling (each keep-out carries its on-screen speed from the lookahead), so a pane is never chased across the view. Once a direction is chosen it is kept while it still clears. A mover that would need a longer slide, or that can only be cleared along its own path, leaves the pane dimmed in place; the pane drops to its light only after the mover has covered more than 60% of it for 300 ms.
