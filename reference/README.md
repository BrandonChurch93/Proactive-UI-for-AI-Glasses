# Reference prototypes

These are the approved prototypes. They are the look and the behavior to match, not code to extend. Open them in a browser at the start, write notes in `STATUS.md`, and open them again before every visual check and every phase review, as the refresh ritual in `CLAUDE.md` says. Read their source only when you need an exact value the docs do not give. Never import from them.

- `hero-block.html` · the approved visual direction. A real-time Three.js view of the Grace Theater block with the glasses card living in it: lighting, post, the dark glass material with the light edge, the light vocabulary (pip, aura, flare, head, leader, seed), the condense motion, the crossing recede. Known deviations from the final spec, already corrected in the docs: it blurs the world when the panel opens (the build must not), it clamps the head turn (the build must not), its people are capsule mannequins, its cars pass through each other, its buildings are symmetrical.
- `walking-rules.html` · the walking rules in motion on the same block. The wearer actually walks; a pace control and seven scenes cover the rules: the speed-scaled focus zone, auto-fold, peripheral entry, head-scan yielding, the pocket and its surfacing at the light, right of way (a truck), backdrop adaptivity, the whisper line, snooze. It has a "Hands busy" toggle that the final rules removed (the dwell ladder replaced it); ignore that toggle. Its pip draws through the truck; the build depth-tests the light vocabulary.
- `card-derby.html` · the three material directions that were judged. Lumen won. Useful for the ladder (pip, glance, card, expanded) and the recede toggle.
- `lumen-study.html` · the full component set as scenes over a painted stand-in: title screen, ask glance, route and turn, map tile, arrival, messages, notifications, reminder, reroute, crossing, translation, landmark, the proactive moments, idle glances. The backdrop is a placeholder; the component behaviors are the reference.

All four load Three.js or GSAP from a CDN and need a network connection to open. `hero-block.html` supports `?still=1&t=<seconds>`; `walking-rules.html` supports `?still=1&scene=<0-6>&t=<seconds>` and exposes `window.__stepTo(t)` for stepping frames from one browser session, the pattern the build's `pnpm shots` should copy. Use these modes to screenshot the reference beats you are judging against, side by side with your own frames.
